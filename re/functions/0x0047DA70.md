## Purpose

Classifies a bounding sphere (`this`, ECX) against one plane (the stack argument):
it computes the signed distance of the sphere's centre to the plane,
`d = dot(plane.normal, centre) - plane.constant`, and returns

- **2** when `d <= -radius`: the sphere lies wholly on the plane's negative side,
- **1** when `d >= radius` (and the first test did not hit): wholly on the positive side,
- **0** otherwise: the sphere straddles the plane, or a NaN reached a comparison.

Two callers loop it over an array of six 16-byte planes under an active-plane
bitmask, and 1 clears that plane's bit in both (no further test needed against it).
They differ on 2: 0x0070DFB0 leaves the loop (0x0070E001 `JE 0x0070E01B`), while
0x007D2EA0 clears its return byte (0x007D2F30) and keeps looping over the remaining
planes (0x007D2F35 `JMP 0x007D2F44`), so later planes can still clear mask bits on
result 1. 0x0049CBF0 tests a single plane (EBP+0x90, 0x0049CCB2) with no loop or
mask. (read, objdump on the md5-matching EXE; see Claims C12-C14.)

**Correction to the seed:** the seed says `ecx = plane` and `stack argument = sphere`.
The instructions say the opposite: the value at **[arg]+0x0C** is subtracted
(FSUB at 0x0047DA90) and **[ECX]+0x0C** is the value compared as `-r` and `r`
(0x0047DA9B, 0x0047DAB3). Two call sites confirm it: ECX is an object's
`+0x20..+0x2C` block and the pushed argument walks a plane array (C12, C13).
The roles in the manifest's "known" text and in knowledge/skinned-vertex-streams.md
("plane against sphere") should be read as "sphere (this) against plane (arg)".

Subsystems touched: none beyond its two 16-byte inputs and its own argument slot.
No clock reads or writes, no globals, no calls. Nothing below it is worth its own
slice; the cost lever (3.8 % of a scene pass, OBVR sampler, measured there, not here)
sits in the callers' six-plane loops (see Open questions).

## Calling convention and parameters

- `__thiscall`: ECX = `this`, the sphere (read: ECX dereferenced at 0x0047DA77,
  0x0047DA7C, 0x0047DA83, 0x0047DA9B, 0x0047DAB3, never loaded inside the function).
- One stack argument at [ESP+4]: pointer to the plane (read: `MOV EAX,[ESP+0x4]`
  at 0x0047DA70).
- Callee cleans: `RET 0x4` on all three exits (0x0047DAB0, 0x0047DAC4, 0x0047DAC9).
- Returns an int in EAX: 2, 1 or 0 (`MOV EAX,0x2` 0x0047DAAB, `MOV EAX,0x1`
  0x0047DABF, `XOR EAX,EAX` 0x0047DAC7).
- General registers changed: EAX only (FNSTSW AX at 0x0047DAA2 / 0x0047DAB8 and the
  return value); ECX, EDX and all callee-saved registers untouched. Also written:
  EFLAGS (TEST at 0x0047DAA4 and 0x0047DABA, XOR at 0x0047DAC7), the x87 condition
  codes (FCOMP at 0x0047DAA0, FCOMPP at 0x0047DAB6) and the argument slot [ESP+4]
  (see below). All of these are caller-volatile (read: the instructions named;
  "caller-volatile" derived from the thiscall convention).
- x87 stack: balanced on every exit (derived from the instruction stack effects:
  return-2 path pops `d` with FSTP ST0 at 0x0047DAA9 after FCOMP popped `-r`;
  the other paths pop `r` and `d` with FCOMPP at 0x0047DAB6). Peak depth 2.
- The argument slot [ESP+4] is reused as a float scratch (written 0x0047DA88 and
  0x0047DA93) after the pointer was copied to EAX. Callee-owned under thiscall, not
  observable to the caller once the slot is popped by RET 4.

## Control flow

Straight-line computation, then two conditional branches. Let `d` be the distance
after the second store (single precision) and `r` = [ECX+0x0C].

| # | at | instruction | condition for the jump | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 0x0047DAA7 | `JNZ 0x0047DAB3` after `FLD r; FCHS; FCOMP` (compares ST0 = `-r` with ST1 = `d`) and `TEST AH,0x1` (C0) | taken when C0 = 1: `-r < d`, or unordered (NaN in `d` or `r`) | not taken (`d <= -r`, ordered): `FSTP ST0`, return **2** (0x0047DAA9..0x0047DAB0) |
| B2 | 0x0047DABD | `JP 0x0047DAC7` after `FLD r; FCOMPP` (compares ST0 = `r` with ST1 = `d`) and `TEST AH,0x41` (C0 + C3) | taken when the masked result has even parity: C0 = C3 = 0 (`r > d`) or C0 = C3 = 1 (unordered) | not taken (exactly one of C0 `r < d` / C3 `r == d`, i.e. `d >= r`): return **1** (0x0047DABF..0x0047DAC4); taken: return **0** (0x0047DAC7..0x0047DAC9) |

Test flows a rebuild needs (each derived from B1/B2):

1. `d < -r` -> 2.
2. `d == -r` -> 2 (equality falls through B1). Includes `r = 0, d = +-0` -> 2 (`-0 == +0`).
3. `-r < d < r` -> 0.
4. `d == r`, `r > 0` -> 1.
5. `d > r` -> 1.
6. `d` NaN -> 0 (B1 taken, B2 taken).
7. `r` NaN -> 0 (same two unordered results).
8. `r < 0`, `d` not NaN -> never 0: `d <= -r` gives 2, else `d > -r > r` gives 1.
   (`r < 0` with `d` NaN gives 0 by flow 6: B1 taken at 0x0047DAA7, B2 taken at
   0x0047DABD on unordered.)
9. `r = +inf`, finite `d` -> 0; `d = +inf`, finite `r` -> 1; `d = -inf`, finite `r` -> 2.
10. Overflow at a store (see C9) -> `d = +-inf` -> 1 or 2 by flow 9.

## Open questions

1. Is this `NiBound::WhichSide(const NiPlane&)` (C16)? Settled by a second source:
   xOBSE/OBVR headers naming NiBound/NiPlane layouts, or a Gamebryo 1.x/2.x reference.
   Until then the name stays a proposal.
2. 0x0070DFD3 is listed as a separate caller entry, but the call at 0x0070DFF9 lies in
   code that 0x0070DFB0 also covers (re/export/rea-all has both 0x70dfb0.c and
   0x70dfd3.c). Is 0x0070DFD3 a second entry into 0x0070DFB0 or a Ghidra split? It
   matters for counting callers; not for this function.
3. The cost lever: the six-plane loops in 0x0070DFB0 (camera/frustum culling,
   planes at +0x2C, mask at +0x8C - role unverified) and 0x007D2EA0 (shadow light range
   test) call this per plane. Worth their own slices: 0x0070DFB0 (the frustum culler,
   likely the bulk of the 3.8 %, unverified - the sampler attributed it to 0x007D2EA0)
   and 0x007D2EA0. A batched rebuild (all active planes at once) must keep the
   per-plane order and the mask update on 1, and each caller's handling of 2:
   0x0070DFB0 exits early (0x0070E001), 0x007D2EA0 clears its return byte and
   continues over the remaining planes (0x007D2F30, 0x007D2F35), still clearing
   mask bits on later 1-results (read, objdump).
4. The game's x87 control word: precision 24-bit is measured (manifest); the exception
   masks (overflow/underflow at the stores, C9) and whether any caller runs on a thread
   with another precision (C11) are not.
5. C8/C9 rest on the Intel SDM statement that precision control rounds only the
   significand; fetch and cite it (SDM vol. 1, "Precision Control Field") before the
   rebuild's edge-case tests are written.

## Addendum 2026-10-30 (architect)

C8/C9 settled by the Intel SDM vol. 1 section 8.1.5.2 "Precision Control Field"
(quoted in knowledge/bone-palettes.md, section "Sphere against plane"): the
single-precision setting reduces the **significand** to 24 bits and affects
only FADD/FSUB/FMUL/FDIV/FSQRT families (FLD, FST, FCOM untouched); nothing in
it reduces the exponent range. So in range the two stores through [ESP+4]
change nothing, and at the edges a product beyond FLT_MAX stays finite in the
register and becomes infinite at the store, one below FLT_MIN is rounded a
second time at the store. Mark: read (SDM), derived (the edge behaviour).

## Addendum 2026-10-30 (rounding control an input)

**The control word at the call site.** The shadow walk 0x007D59E0 calls
IsInRange 0x007D2EA0, and through it this function, with FCW **0x0C7F**:
precision 24 bits, rounding control (bits 10-11) = 11, round toward zero
(measured: FXSAVE at the walk's entry, artifacts/shadow/obng-range-walk-routed/
20261030-131826/OBNG.log, the MISMATCH lines). The function loads no control
word of its own (read: no FLDCW/FNINIT/FLDENV/FXRSTOR in its 32 instructions),
so it rounds under its caller's. The rounding control applies to every FMUL,
FADDP and FSUB result and to both FSTP float (Intel SDM vol. 1 4.8.4 and
8.1.5.3; measured below).

**The mode is an input of the rebuild.** `which_side::X87Rounding` {Nearest,
Down, Up, Zero} from `RoundingFromControlWord(FCW)`; `SignedDistance(plane,
sphere, mode)`, `WhichSide(sphere, plane, mode)` and the engine shape
`engine::WhichSide(bound, plane, mode)` take it, with no default
(src/engine/render/NiBound_WhichSide{Logic,}.h). `Classify` takes none: FCHS
and FCOM/FCOMPP do not round, so the classification of two stored floats is
the same under every mode (derived). Three points the directed modes forced
on the 24-bit model (all measured, tests/X87RoundingHostTest.cpp on the host
x87 and the emulator cases below):
- a sum is held as a double and its exact error (TwoSum) before the 24-bit
  rounding - the double sum alone drops the sticky bit a directed mode needs
  (1 + 2^-80 is 1 + 2^-23 under Up, the double sum is 1.0; case R3);
- an exactly zero sum is -0 under Down, +0 otherwise (IEEE 754 6.3);
- the store converts under the mode: overflow gives +-inf at nearest,
  +-FLT_MAX toward zero, FLT_MAX/-inf down, +inf/-FLT_MAX up; denormal results
  round to the 2^-149 quantum under the mode.

**Emulator cases per mode** (tools/difftest/cases/0x0047DA70-rounding.json from
tools/difftest/generators/0x0047DA70-rounding.py; results
tools/difftest/expected/0x0047DA70-rounding.json, measured in Unicorn 2026-10-30):
the 23 cases E1..E24 (E18 apart) at fpcw 0x047F, 0x087F and 0x0C7F, and R1..R15
(ties and sticky bits in the dot product, overflow at either store, denormal
stores, negative ties, the FSUB's rounding, exact cancellation) at all four -
129 cases: 15 at 0x007F, 38 each at 0x047F, 0x087F, 0x0C7F. The rebuild equals
the original's EAX in all 129 (tests/NiBound_WhichSideTest.cpp,
DifferentialRounding); in 22 of them the mode decides the answer, so a
nearest-only rebuild fails those. The same 129 inputs gave the same EAX on the
host's own x87 running the original's 92 bytes (scratch program, not kept),
and 20 million random and edge inputs (5 million per mode) gave 0 mismatches
between the original bytes on the host x87 and the rebuild, where the
nearest-only rebuild differed on 0.57 / 0.60 / 0.90 million of the down / up /
truncate inputs (scratch program, not kept). State: verified (emulator) under
the four rounding controls at precision 24; the in-game check under 0x0C7F is
the range walk's rebuild-first shadow run, not yet run with this rebuild.
