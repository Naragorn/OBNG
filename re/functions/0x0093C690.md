## Purpose

A GJK-style closest-point iteration between two convex shapes, A in its own frame and B placed
by a transform. It keeps a simplex of up to 4 support vertices per shape inside `this`
(the agent's cache), reduces the simplex by the case pair (dimA, dimB) through a jump table,
asks each shape for a new support vertex along the current separating direction
(shape vtable +0x24), stops when no shape adds a vertex, then normalises the separating
direction and writes it with the signed distance in w to the caller's output.

It reads no clock, takes no lock, calls no allocator and touches no TLS (read: no `FS:`
access, no call outside the five direct callees and the three virtual call sites 0x0093CC24,
0x0093D084, 0x0093D21E, asm.s).

## Claims

1. It is a GJK/GSK-type closest-point walk over two simplices of at most 4 vertices each, with
   per-case reducers. **derived** from the (dimA,dimB) dispatch at 0x0093C741-0x0093C767, the
   support queries at 0x0093CC24/0x0093D084/0x0093D21E and the final normalisation.
2. B's vertices are kept twice, in B's frame (+0xE0) and in A's frame (+0xA0), linked by arg3.
   **read** 0x0093C6D0-0x0093C703, 0x0093D17D-0x0093D1BF.
3. The only memory written is `this`, arg4 and the stack frame. **read** (every store in asm.s
   targets ESI-, EDI(=ESI+4)-, EBX(=ESI+0x20 or ESI+0xA0)-, ESP- or [EBP+0x14]-based addresses).
4. No global, singleton or TLS write, no clock read, no allocation in this body. **read**
5. No iteration cap in either loop. **read**: no counter compare in asm.s. The outer loop returns
   to 0x0093C722 only after a vertex was added: its backward jumps are 0x0093CD24, 0x0093D1CC and
   0x0093D310, each after INC dim (0x0093CD1A, 0x0093D1C3, 0x0093D306). The inner re-dispatch
   loop 0x0093CB14 JBE 0x0093C760 has no counter either; it ends only when a reducer lowers
   dimA+dimB or leaves through the join. (The other backward jumps, 0x0093CEAC/0x0093CFBA/0x0093CFEE
   to the join 0x0093CB1F and 0x0093C70D in the prologue loop, do not form loops of their own.)
   That both loops always terminate is **unverified**: it depends on the return values of
   0x0093B220, 0x0093B000, 0x0093C0C0 and 0x008D1A30 (unfollowed; see step 3 (2,2) for one
   value that would re-dispatch without progress) and on the outer dims being bounded by
   +0x08/+0x0C.
6. The progress and termination tests use x87 (FLD/FMUL/FCOMP) on SSE-computed floats, so results
   depend on each thread's x87 control word and MXCSR. **derived** from 0x0093CB9F-0x0093CBCB and
   the SSE arithmetic.
7. No guard on nn before RSQRTSS in the finish (0x0093D335-0x0093D370, **read**). With nn == 0,
   RSQRTSS gives +inf and the Newton step (nn·r² = 0·inf) gives NaN, so the written normal and
   distance are NaN (**derived** from IEEE semantics of the instructions read). The finish can see
   nn == 0 only after the 0x0093C350 call (nn < eps leaves through 0x0093CBCB to 0x0093D317, and
   the no-progress exit at 0x0093D1D1 is reached only after both tests passed with nn >= eps),
   **derived**; whether 0x0093C350 can leave +0x120 zero is **unverified**.
8. Callers: 0x0093D4A0, 0x0093F1C0, 0x008FF1C0, 0x0093DE40, 0x0093F800, 0x0093F250, 0x0093F5E0
   (meta.json). 0x0093F250 is the "Gsk" agent per knowledge/threading.md:244. **read** (list)

## Could two threads run this at once for two different phantoms?

For this body: yes, provided each thread passes its own `this` cache and its own output vector.
It writes nothing outside them and its frame and never takes a lock or allocates (**read**,
claims 3 and 4). The globals it reads (0x00A99F04..0E, 0x00A965C0) are not written by this body
(**read**); that they are constant everywhere is **derived** (see (d)). What stands against it, all
outside this body: (1) the cache must not be shared - if two phantoms' agents could point at
the same cache (one pair seen from both sides), the writes race (**unverified** who owns the
cache: the callers, chiefly 0x0093F250); (2) the shape's vtable +0x24 implementations and the
four direct callees are unfollowed - any cache, counter or allocation in them decides
(**unverified**; 0x0093C350 -> 0x00952B90 reads DAT_00A9610C); (3) each worker thread needs the
same x87/MXCSR state as the main thread for identical results (**derived**, claim 6); (4) the
caller-owned inputs it reads must not be written concurrently during the call: arg3, the
transform (0x0093C6A0-0x0093C6AE, 0x0093D00B-0x0093D01C, 0x0093D180-0x0093D1AD, passed again to
0x0093C350 at 0x0093D322), and the shapes' vtable pointers (0x0093CC06, 0x0093D023, 0x0093D206) -
for example a body's transform update on another thread (**unverified** whether that can happen).
